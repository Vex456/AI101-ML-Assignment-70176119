Pool A • Numerical Regression

Why Can't a Simple Line Predict Concrete Strength?

Imagine mixing cement, water, fly ash, and gravel. In civil engineering, testing a concrete block's compressive strength takes up to 28 days in a curing water bath. We want an AI that predicts the final strength (in MegaPascals, MPa) in seconds.

⚠️
The Trap (Why Linear Fails):

Concrete hydration is a non-linear chemical reaction. Strength shoots up quickly during the first 7 days, then levels off like a logarithm. A straight line either grossly overshoots young concrete or predicts negative strength!

💡
The Secret Weapon (Feature Engineering):

By giving the model log(Age) and the physics-based Water / Cement ratio (Abrams' Law), our models instantly jump from mediocre to laboratory-grade accuracy!

Question 1

Why does the linear baseline fail?

The Gauss-Markov assumption of linearity is violated. Strength grows with $\ln(\text{Age})$, meaning initial slope is massive and decays over time. Linear regression treats Day 1 to 2 the same as Day 100 to 101!

Question 2

Did engineered features help all models?

Linear and Ridge saw dramatic gains ($R^2$ jumping from ~0.60 to ~0.83). Tree models already partition step-wise, so their gain was smaller, but $\frac{w}{c}$ explicitly unifies two correlated splits into one master physical feature!

Question 3

Where are the largest residual errors?

Largest residuals occur on young samples ($\le 7$ days) and extreme blast-furnace slag mixes, where hydration kinetics vary drastically based on ambient temperature and initial chemical acceleration.

The Fake Job Posting Detective & The Ablation Truth

In the EMSCAD dataset (17,880 postings), only 866 jobs (~4.8%) are fraudulent. If a dumb model labels literally every single job as "Real", it gets a dazzling 95.2% Accuracy—while letting every scammer slip through!

💡 Core Lesson: Why the assignment forces an Ablation Study: We must prove whether text descriptions alone beat structured metadata (company logo, telecommuting, salary), or if combining them creates an unbeatable fraud shield.

Pool C • Sequence-to-Sequence NLP

Why Does Word-for-Word Translation Fail for Urdu?

English is a Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) language: "The boy [S] kicked [V] the ball [O]". Urdu is strictly Subject-Object-Verb (SOV): "لڑکے نے گیند کو لات ماری" (The boy [S] the ball [O] kicked [V]).

🧠 The Attention Mechanism: Instead of squashing a whole English sentence into one static vector, Bahdanau/Luong attention acts like a magnifying glass, looking back at the exact source word needed as each Urdu word is generated!
